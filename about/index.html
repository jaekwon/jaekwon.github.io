Tendermint

In 2014 I published Tendermint: Consensus without Mining, the first protocol to secure a proof-of-stake blockchain with classical Byzantine fault tolerant consensus. Validators post a bond that is destroyed if they sign conflicting blocks, so equivocation is punishable rather than merely unrewarded. That combination — BFT agreement plus bonded, slashable stake — is what makes proof of stake work, and it is the design later systems adopted.

“Repurposing BFT algorithms for proof of stake was first introduced by Tendermint, and has modern inspirations such as [Algorand]. Casper follows this BFT tradition, though with some modifications.”

Vitalik Buterin and Virgil Griffith, Casper the Friendly Finality Gadget (2017), the paper specifying Ethereum's proof of stake.

The protocol's formal specification, with safety and liveness proofs, is Buchman, Kwon and Milosevic, The latest gossip on BFT consensus (2018). Development history is public at github.com/tendermint/tendermint (repository created May 2014).
